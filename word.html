Ulrich Keutchatang · Pexels

They Call It Writing

I write as I breathe.
Involuntarily.
Without it, I die.

I write to survive.
I write to live
and I live to write.

But words hurt.

They enter the blood.
They lodge themselves in the cells.
I carry them
in every molecule,
every atom.

I feed on them.
They feed on me.

An osmosis
of poetry and bone.

They seep into me
until I swell with them.

I am impregnated by language.

The words grow organs.
They borrow my blood.
They press against my ribs.
They kick.

I carry them.
I never come to terms with them.
I carry them
until carrying becomes impossible.

Until they tear through my flesh—

placenta-covered prose,
blood-stained poetry,
utter madness.

They call it writing.
