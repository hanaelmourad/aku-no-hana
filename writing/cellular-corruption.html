FRAGMENT / 2026

Cellular Corruption

Bad blood to the last molecule and atom.

A species of war and mutual annihilation.

Corrupt DNA architecture.
Inherited over millennia.
Biological malware we mistake for instinct,
viral cell growth we mistake for evolution.

What kind of evolutionary chain results in a species
this good at extraction, devastation, and denial?

You cannot bioengineer this level of stooping so low as a species.
It has to come from divine intervention.
In celestial labs,
beyond the event horizon—

where gods go to experiment with dark jokes.

Our blueprints are defective by design.
We’re born already complicit.

I,
too,
am complicit.

I adopt plants and bring them into my home.
I control their destiny.
I decide who gets
the sunny window,
the nice pot,
the right resources and attention,
and who gets what is subpar, sub-everything.

I curate their lives like some micro-colonial botanist,
pretending to bring them freedom and a better life.

A small-scale god,
all in small caps.

I recycle.
I compost.
I eat less red meat.
I walk to the store and carry reusable bags.

I eat bio for my microbiome, which I previously went all Hiroshima on with antibiotics.

I offer my body apologies in the form of sauerkraut, Greek yogurt, and fermented things in expensive little jars.

But I also chain-smoke half the week,
order Amazon with next-day delivery,
and throw out half of last week’s groceries because I can't gather the will to cook.

I am a hypocrite—

I’m a symptom.

The earth doesn’t need people like me.

But I’m still here.

A walking carbon footprint.
A wasteful animal with expensive desires and no self-discipline.
A consumer of things and moments and people.

Still pretending I can love plants like children,
while letting their leaves brown and curl when I dissociate.

Even my good intentions are wrapped in plastic.

I used to believe I could opt out of the damage.
That my smallness made me innocent.

But I know now that I am the damage.
I live it.
I exhale it.
I perform it daily.

A woman-shaped virus
wearing good intentions as camouflage.

In the mirror,
I see a participant.
One of billions.

Another cell in the organism
eating its own host.

I’m complicit.
I’m guilty.
My hands are dirty.

Bad species.
Bad blood.
