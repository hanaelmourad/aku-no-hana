FRAGMENT / 2026

My Body Is a Weather Report

Summer stretched into October.

Plastic multiplied.
Forests burned.
Cities flooded.

Wars streamed live
into the palm of my hand.

My hair dried first.

Then the plants.

I don't remember which one of us
stopped drinking first.

The leaves browned at the edges.
So did I.

Drought damned existence.

My neck hardened.

My shoulders climbed towards my ears
until the muscles became stone.

Pressure gathered behind my eyes.

Migraines so bright
I had to shut every curtain,
turn off every sound,
silence everything.

Even my mind.

I couldn't listen to the dog breathe.

I shushed her.

Nausea twirled my entire existence
like an untrained Sufi
in mawlawi dance.

Some days
I dragged the weight of a planet around.

Heavy.
Swollen.
Pulled down by my own gravity.

Other days
my extremities disappeared into oblivion.

Fingers first.
Then feet.

I became dark around the edges.

I became ill with everything
and nothing.

I visited my doctors
more than I took my dog for a walk.

An entire system failure
with no diagnosis.

The symptoms came closer.
The diagnosis moved farther away.

My body started shutting down
part by part.

The brain wasn't cooperating either.

I couldn't accomplish
the most menial tasks.

Dishes fossilised in the sink.
Laundry fermented in the machine.
Dead leaves collected beneath the pots.

Home turned into a war zone.

My body too.

My hair became so dry
the plants begged to share the water.

Everything needed watering.

The apartment shrank. The days shrank. I shrank.

I became heavy and curled at the top,
my neck sinking between my shoulders.

Gravity slowly rearranging me
into a shape closer to the ground.

Somewhere behind my forehead
orders are being given.

The body obeys-
my stomach spits acid and flames,
my jaw locks,
my hands go numb in solidarity.

Platoons
plotting,
attacking,
withholding.

Until there isn't much left of me.

A mass of biological mess,
A little flesh.
A little pulse.
A little consciousness.
A lot of pain.

Something almost human.

There is poetry
in what remains.
