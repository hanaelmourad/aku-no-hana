Dumb Human

I automated everything.

Not because I’m a cool millennial
but because my muscles are too tired
to do repetitive tasks.

Give me motors and engines.
Give me microchips.

The toothbrush spins on its own.
The face brush whirrs in circles.
Smart plugs,
smartphones,
smart lights,
smart home—

dumb human.

Machines remember
so I don’t have to.

They wake me up.
Count my steps.
Tell me when to stand,
when to breathe,
when to sleep.

The hum of the electronics is relentless.

A low-grade tinnitus that never heals.

Sometimes it’s subtle.

Other times it bursts into roars,
drilling through my ears
and my thoughts.

Part human,
part machine.

Already a cyborg.

The phone is part of me.

A limb.

Not the worst of limbs.

It tugs at my neck
every time I look up.

My shoulders curve around it.

My spine remembers its shape.

It sleeps next to me.

Sometimes under my pillow.

5G
nestled into my dreams.

I wake up
and reach for it
before I reach for myself.

I automated everything.
